# Regulatory context: beneficial ownership law and policy, as of 24 November 2026

Load this file for questions about the EU AML package and its deadlines, 6AMLD transposition and the September 2026 infringements, CJEU case law, UK rules, the Crown Dependencies and Overseas Territories, other jurisdictions (Switzerland, Japan, Korea, the US, Latin America), sanctions ownership ("50%") rules, FATF wording, and verification and discrepancy reporting.

**How to use it.** Every fact carries a date and, where one exists, an official source. Law moves. Before stating a status as current, check the date here against today. For a single country's register access, read `bo-access-status.md` first; it is the dated snapshot of the Beneficial ownership access status database and has more detail per country than this file. Where a fact rests on a press report of an official text rather than the text itself, it says so.

**Wording rules Stephen uses** (adopted 16 and 19 Nov 2026):
- FATF, the EU and AMLA **expect** ownership information to be verified against other reliable sources, including public registers. Never say they "require cross-checking". FATF INR.10 says the data "may be obtained from a public register, from the customer or from other reliable sources"; AMLR Art 22(7) and AMLA's draft RTS Art 8 test ("consistent with other sources") support "expect", not a hard mandate.
- FATF and the World Bank "point to" or "recognise" a standard such as BODS. Never "endorse".
- Describe what a register publishes and since when. Do not judge a jurisdiction ("secrecy", "haven", "opaque").

## 1. The EU AML package (2024)

| Instrument | What it does | Key dates |
|---|---|---|
| **AMLR**, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 | The single rulebook for obliged entities, including the beneficial ownership definition (≥25% of shares, voting rights or ownership interest, direct or indirect; Art 52) and CDD | Applies **10 Jul 2027** |
| **AMLD6**, Directive (EU) 2024/1640 | What member states run: FIUs, supervisors, central registers including BO registers and their access rules | Staged: **Art 74** by 10 Jul 2025; **Arts 11, 12, 13, 15** (access) by **10 Jul 2026**; most of the rest by 10 Jul 2027 |
| **AMLAR**, Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 | Creates the Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA), Frankfurt | AMLA began operating in 2025; direct supervision of selected obliged entities from 2028 |

Links: [AMLR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1624/oj/eng) · [AMLD6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/1640/oj/eng) · [AMLAR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1620/oj/eng)

### AMLD6 access, in one paragraph
After *WM and Sovim* (below) the package rebuilt access around **legitimate interest**. Persons presumed to have one include journalists, civil society organisations and academics working on money laundering, and persons likely to enter into a transaction with the entity. The directive sets the procedure: a decision within a fixed number of working days (12 is the figure several states have adopted), approval valid for a set period, and mutual recognition of another member state's approval. Art 74 was the bridge: it amended AMLD5 so that a legitimate-interest route existed in law from 10 Jul 2025 while the detailed rules were transposed.

### Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2354: BO submission formats
- Published **22 Oct 2026**, in force **11 Nov 2026**, applies from **10 Jul 2027**.
- Sets the formats in which BO information is submitted to central registers.
- Stephen's analysis: 66 requirements; **33 map to BODS directly, 22 in part, 11 not at all**. Post: [The EU has written down what a beneficial ownership data standard must hold](https://stephenabbottpugh.medium.com/the-eu-has-written-down-what-a-beneficial-ownership-data-standard-must-hold-9d59b015fa11).
- The regulation describes content, not a serialisation. No standard is named. That is the opening for BODS.

### AMLA's draft CDD RTS (AMLR Art 28(1))
- Final draft published **1 Oct 2026**; still a draft until the Commission adopts it.
- Art 11(2) lists six data points to identify per entity; Art 11(4) lists four elements of a complex structure; Art 8 sets the reliability test for sources ("consistent with other sources"); Art 19 and recital 12 cover verification.
- Stephen's analysis: 57 requirements; **34 map to BODS directly, 13 in part, 10 not at all**. Post: [AMLA has listed the fields. Nobody has named the format](https://stephenabbottpugh.medium.com/amla-has-listed-the-fields-nobody-has-named-the-format-2c8c8da1c48a).

### BORIS (interconnection of BO registers)
Required by AMLD5 and carried into AMLD6. Coverage depends on each national register; where a register is closed to the public, BORIS gives no public route either. See the BORIS section of SKILL.md.

## 3. CJEU case law

- **WM and Sovim**, joined cases **C-37/20 and C-601/20**, judgment **22 Nov 2022**. General public access to BO registers under AMLD5 Art 30(5)(c) was held invalid as a disproportionate interference with Arts 7 and 8 of the Charter. Several registers (Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Austria, Belgium and others) closed to the public within days. The 2024 package's legitimate-interest model is the response.
- **C-609/26, Luxembourg Business Registers II** (pending). Reference from the Luxembourg District Court, order of **29 May 2026**. A UAE head of state's company asked the LBR to restrict access to his RBE entry for disproportionate risk; refused. Questions: (1) are "exceptional circumstances" limited to the listed risks, and must a specific, genuine, current risk be proved; (2) can access be restricted when the data is public elsewhere; (3) is disproportionate risk assessed individually or by category, and do State security interests count. No AG opinion or judgment as of 20 Nov 2026. [Case page](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/affair?lang=EN&searchTerm=%2522C%252D609%252F26%2522&publishedId=C-609%2F26). Cite only the official CJEU record, never law-firm commentary.
- Do not confuse with C-626/25 (a Brussels I case) or the Luxembourg Cour de cassation's 12 Nov 2026 RBE criminal-liability ruling.

## 4. United Kingdom

- **PSC register** (Companies House) is public, free and available as bulk data and API since 2016. Threshold: more than 25%.
- **ECCTA 2023 identity verification**: mandatory for new directors and PSCs from **18 Nov 2025**, with a transition for existing ones. Verified status shows on the register.
- **MLR amendment 2026**: The Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Regulations 2026, in force **30 Jun 2026** (reg 19), changed MLR reg 33(1)(b). Mandatory enhanced due diligence now applies only to persons established in a FATF "High-Risk Jurisdiction subject to a Call for Action" (the black list). The grey list is a geographic risk factor under reg 33(6)(c). There is no separate UK high-risk third countries schedule. HM Treasury's Money Laundering Advisory Notice restates the FATF lists after each plenary (October 2026 edition published 11 Nov 2026).
- **BODS**: the gov.uk open standards page for BODS still names **v0.3** (checked Nov 2026); v0.4 is current.
- **Register of Overseas Entities** (ECTEA 2022): public, with trust information available on request since 2023.

## 6. Other jurisdictions

- **Switzerland**: federal transparency register for legal entities launched **1 Oct 2026**; authorities only, no public access.
- **Japan**: a BO register bill was submitted to the Diet on **23 Oct 2026**. Until then, the Ministry of Justice's voluntary BO information list scheme (since 2022) is the only route.
- **South Korea**: KoFIU has a plan only; no register. Open DART publishes listed-company filings.
- **United States**: FinCEN no longer collects BO information from domestic companies under the Corporate Transparency Act (interim final rule of March 2025 exempting US companies and US persons; foreign reporting companies remain). The register was never public.
- **Canada**: federal ISC register public since Jan 2024 (Corporations Canada); provinces vary.
- **Latin America**: CEFILAT/FTC, *The Invisible Owners* (Aug 2026), covers 17 countries (mainland Latin America without Venezuela, plus Belize). Eleven have a BO register (AR, BZ, BR, CO, CR, EC, NI, PA, PY, PE, UY); **all eleven are open only to the state and obliged entities**. Chile has no BO register but ChileCompra collects supplier BO declarations under **Ley 21.634**; Mexico has BO obligations in tax law (CFF) without a public register.
- **Africa**: Nigeria's CAC BO register is public (2023), the first in Africa; Botswana's register (CIPA, 10% threshold) is public but CIPA **refused permission for republication on 30 Oct 2026**, so its data must not be reused.
- **Australia**: no BO register; Treasury consultations continue. ASIC consultation CP391 (Stephen submitted a response on 13 Oct 2026).

## 7. Sanctions ownership ("50%") rules

These are ownership tests for whether a company is treated as sanctioned because of who owns it. They are not BO definitions. Control tests run alongside and are a matter of judgment, not arithmetic.

| Regime | Test | Aggregation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US OFAC** | Owned **50% or more**, directly or indirectly, by one or more blocked persons | **Aggregated** across blocked persons; a caught company's holdings count in full (FAQ 401 worked examples; FAQ 399) | OFAC 50 Percent Rule guidance (2014) and FAQs 398–402 |
| **EU** | **50% or more** of the proprietary rights, or control (as OpenCheck reads the Council's Best Practices and the Commission's FAQs; check the current wording before relying on the boundary case of exactly 50%) | Aggregated across designated persons | EU Best Practices for the effective implementation of restrictive measures; Commission consolidated FAQs |
| **UK** | **More than 50%** of shares or voting rights, held by one designated person directly or through companies it owns more than 50% of, or control | **Per designated person**, no aggregation across designated persons | OFSI General Guidance, ownership and control |
| **Canada** | **50% or more** of shares or voting rights, or power to change the board, or to direct activities | Aggregated (GAC guidance page created **14 Aug 2026**); voting rights are a separate limb, never added to shares | SEMA s.2.1, JVCFOA s.2.01 (S.C. 2023 c.26 s.253) |

Points that matter for data work:
- Integrated ownership (multiplying along a chain) is **not** how the 50% rules work. Under OFAC and the EU, once a company is caught, everything it owns counts in full (T-Rank's 50% rule, Eqs 1–5).
- A name-only match to a sanctions list is evidence to weigh, never a party to sum.
- Ended holdings are out of scope for a current test (OFAC FAQ 402).
- OpenCheck computes the ownership limbs only (Phase 342; Canada added Nov 2026) and prints that control is not computed.

## 8. FATF

- **Recommendation 24** (legal persons) revised **March 2022**: multi-pronged approach, a register or alternative mechanism, adequate, accurate and up-to-date information, timely access. Guidance March 2023.
- **Recommendation 25** (legal arrangements) revised **February 2023**; guidance March 2024.
- **INR.10**: BO information "may be obtained from a public register, from the customer or from other reliable sources". Reliance on a register alone is not enough; the obliged entity is expected to verify.
- **Low-capacity countries**: on **18 Nov 2026** FATF set out a new approach for low-capacity countries, framing AML/CFT within domestic revenue and development goals. In the least developed countries, 84% of assessed immediate outcomes were rated low effectiveness. Stephen's reading: reuse is the saving; data collected once in an open standard serves the FIU, tax authority, procurement and EITI. Post: [Cheaper by design: open standards when aid budgets shrink](https://stephenabbottpugh.medium.com/cheaper-by-design-open-standards-when-aid-budgets-shrink-689208188404) (20 Nov 2026).
- Plenaries publish the black list ("High-Risk Jurisdictions subject to a Call for Action") and grey list ("Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring") three times a year; the UK, the EU and AMLA each read those lists differently (UK: black list only for mandatory EDD since 30 Jun 2026).

## 9. Verification and discrepancy reporting

- **AMLD5 Art 30(4)** (carried into AMLD6): obliged entities and competent authorities report discrepancies between the register and the information they hold. Implemented nationally, for example:
  - **UK**: the PSC discrepancy reporting duty (MLR reg 30A) for obliged entities; Companies House's own route for anyone else.
  - **Estonia**: a discrepancy flag is published in the open data (`lahknevusteade_esitatud`); the report itself is not.
  - **Denmark, Norway, Latvia**: routes through the business registers (see `bo-access-status.md`, Discrepancy route column, drafted 3 Nov 2026 and marked unverified until Stephen checks them).
- **ECCTA (UK)** and **AMLD6** move registers towards verifying what is filed, not only collecting it.
- A record that disagrees with another register is not a beneficial ownership discrepancy unless the disputed field is BO information. Keep the two apart. OpenCheck's discrepancy note (Nov 2026) is a register-record note and says it is not an AMLR or PSC BO discrepancy report.

## 10. Evidence from Stephen's 2026 testing

- **Estonia** ([post](https://stephenabbottpugh.medium.com/testing-estonias-beneficial-ownership-register-against-its-dbfb54c51702)): in **95.1% of 20,155** companies, the declared beneficial owners match the people the shareholder list points to when ownership is integrated along the chain (T-Rank method). Norway's comparable figure is **84%**.
- **UK PSC vs the OECD-UNSD MEIP Global Register** ([post](https://stephenabbottpugh.medium.com/what-an-open-ownership-register-adds-testing-the-uk-psc-register-against-the-oecds-global-register-b3e7374e3bbe)): the PSC chain places **91.7%** of MEIP's unknown UK subsidiaries in the same group, and **69.3%** reach the group head.
- These figures support the argument that open, structured shareholder and BO data can be checked against itself. They do not show that every declaration is true.
